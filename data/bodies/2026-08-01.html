Plus, in this week’s Installer: Spider-Man is back again, a cozy game worth trying, Elon roasts, and much more.

Hi, friends! Welcome to Installer No. 138, your guide to the best and Verge-iest stuff in the world. (If you’re new here, welcome, happy August, and also you can read all the old editions at the Installer homepage.)

This week, I’ve been reading about microwave sounds and Meta glasses and Alex vs. Alix, testing the Logitech Alto Keys K98M to see if I’m ready for the mechanical keyboard life, digging into the history of the first chatbot, clearing space on my desk for a Bob Ross deskmat, doing a full Ted Lasso rewatch ahead of the new season next week, finally getting into Cyberpunk 2077 now that it’s so cheap, cackling and nodding at this Adobe hate post, and upgrading my home camera setup with the truly enormous Elgato Prompter XL.

Also, I have some personal news: I’m having a baby! (If your response to this is, Didn’t you just have like 37 other babies, yes, yes I did. Pray for me.) I’m planning to be out from about mid-August to the beginning of October, but I’m also in could-happen-anytime land now. So if I’m suddenly gone, that’s why! Just like last year, my colleague Jay Peters will be taking over Installer while I’m out, and just like last year, he’s going to be great.

But for now, I’m still here! I also have for you a new superhero movie, a new take on an old game, some great Musk-related comedy, and much more. Let’s do it.

(As always, the best part of Installer is your ideas and tips. What are you watching / reading / playing / listening to / putting in a blender this week? Tell me everything: installer@theverge.com. And if you know someone else who might enjoy Installer, forward it to them and tell them to subscribe here.)

The Drop

  • Spider-Man: Brand New Day. The Tom Holland Spidey era just never seems to miss. It’s pretty impressive to pull off a more or less full story reboot while keeping all the same actors and characters, but by all accounts this one pulls it off. Since I still can’t get IMAX Odyssey tickets, this one’s at the top of my to-watch list.
  • Pastebot 3. Missed this one last week! Great clipboard managers are right up there with launchers and text expanders in the “how did I ever use a computer without this” category, and Pastebot is one of the Mac’s best. The new version has smart organization options, support for all kinds of media, and much more. The kind of app you’ll find new uses for… forever.
  • President Curtis. I was going to describe this as “basically a Rick and Morty spinoff” before I realized it’s actually a Rick and Morty spinoff. Perhaps unsurprisingly, people seem to either love it or hate it. I think most of us would agree R&M is not what it once was, but the idea of throwing all its chaotic energy into the US government does pay off at least a few times. And it’s not not political commentary, you know?
  • “Why Is So Much AI Tech Useless?” Two of my favorite people on the internet — Joanna Stern and Hasan Minhaj — go deep on where AI is, and isn’t, actually good and useful in our lives. Minhaj’s complete disbelief at all these overhyped products just gives me life.
  • Halo: Campaign Evolved. Confession: I was always terrible at Halo, and I’ve always been unfairly mad at the series because of it. The new remake appears to be a mediocre game (with terrible dialogue) but great nostalgia bait, and that might be enough for a lot of people looking to get Master Chief back into their lives.
  • Leafy Corner. This game exists somewhere on the spectrum between Animal Crossing and RollerCoaster Tycoon, and I mean both as a compliment. It’s a quiet, cozy game, but also something of a management sim, all in the confines of a plant shop. Adorable.
  • Spotify Running Mode. I actually think Spotify’s last take on running, with adaptive tempos and generative beats, was both cooler and a more interesting take on AI. But Spotify’s prompted playlists have generally been pretty good, and I appreciate having the timing updates built in instead of having some other app constantly ducking my music to tell me I’m too slow.
  • “Elon’s Epic Ego Explained.” Nobody does topical comedy like Josh Johnson, and this is my favorite of his recent sets. It’s not just 45 minutes of making fun of Musk — it’s also about The Odyssey and a bunch of other things — but it’s both ruthless and right about the world’s richest man.
  • The SwitchBot Battery Circulator Fan 2 Pro. My colleague Thomas Ricker has successfully convinced me that SwitchBot’s fans are worth a look over the “whatever’s on the shelf at Target” models I’ve been buying for years. This new $120 model is quiet and powerful, charges over USB-C, and is going to live on my patio the rest of the summer.

Screen share

I buried the lede here: The biggest new thing of the week, at least in my book, is Instapaper 10. Instapaper is one of the original read-later apps, and has continued to be among the best on the market. After some weird ownership stuff over the years, Instapaper has been in the hands of Brian Donohue, who has been steadily improving the service in lots of ways. This week’s launch comes with a really nice redesign, a way better web app, and a lot of helpful organization and reading tools. I’ve been testing it, and I think it’s back to being my go-to recommendation for reading apps. (For power users I still think Readwise is the move.)

With the new app out, I asked Brian to share his homescreen with us — I wanted to see how he organized his stuff, and if he might be working on even more cool stuff. Turns out he is! Here’s his homescreen, plus some info on the apps he uses and why:

The phone: iPhone 16 Pro. I almost always purchase iPhones every other generation. Maybe a foldable this year?

The wallpaper: My daughter, as a reminder that my apps must be good otherwise she will not have food on the table.

The apps: Safari, Google Calendar, Emerald, Google Maps, Weather, Photos, Instapaper, Kobo Books, Reddit, 1Password, Google Authenticator, Slack, Google Docs, Google Sheets, ForeFlight, Flight Circle, Claude, Linear, Phone, Sunflower, Messages, Spotify.

I’ll start with the ones not yet in the App Store: Emerald and Sunflower.

Emerald is a Markdown editor I’m working on that solves two personal pain points: native iOS / macOS client, and collaboration on markdown files. I have been a big Obsidian user the past two years, but it’s been failing me in those areas. I’ve been writing with Emerald exclusively for the past few months and it’s almost ready for beta testing. Waitlist in the link.

Sunflower is a new email client from Bobby Goodlatte and friends. It’s beautifully designed and has some tasteful AI features, like summarizing your inbox, chatting with your email, and an Autopilot feature which seems to be perpetually “Coming Soon.” I’ve been trying a few clients in the “AI email” space like Extra, and an autopilot tool called Cora, which didn’t stick.

Instapaper’s big launch this week included a new home screen widget. It fits nicely in the widget stack alongside the Apple News, Stocks, and Weather widgets.

Additionally, you have many of the usual suspects. I am doing a huge amount of my work with Claude and Linear these days, both of which have fantastic iOS apps.

I also asked Brian to share a few things he’s into right now. Here’s what he sent back:

  • I’ve always been inspired by creative builders, and two come to mind right now. The first is Andrew Jones, who has been building free & open-source alternatives to paid software (e.g. Granola, Wispr Flow) — fingers crossed Instapaper is not next. The second is Q, a designer who has been building small, single-purpose websites for himself that I find inspiring. It almost reminds me of a purer, distant version of the Internet, where you built a bunch of Geocities pages for fun and without expectations of fame and fortune.
  • Hyper Mesh. Q has been exploring image reveal animations, and built a tool to tweak presets on the initial mask and the animation that reveals the image.
  • SVG Star Editor. Create a star from different settings and export to SVG.
  • Chess Moves. Q is learning how to play chess with his girlfriend and made this tutorial website as a companion tool.

Signing off

The coolest internet experience I had this week was scrolling through the Everything Machine, a deep and detailed breakdown of how your phone works. It’s only the first chapter of what I hope will be a very long exploration of how smartphones changed everything, and I love the way it seems to interact with your phone as you scroll. Our phones are such a “This is water” thing in our lives now, and I really enjoy anything that makes me pay a little more attention to the object, the cultural force, and the economic engine that takes up so much of my life. Highly recommend taking a scroll.
